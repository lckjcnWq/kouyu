# Go / Web3 technical-interview curriculum

This release defines **18 simulated English technical-interview scenes**, with exactly three scenes in each of six tracks. It is a curriculum for the existing preview → readiness → role-play → report → archive flow, not a second application or an automated hiring assessment.

## Contents and scope

- `tracks.json` registers the six tracks in the required order.
- `concepts.json` registers 24 reusable technical concepts. A concept describes a contextual observation, not a credential.
- `scenes/*.json` contains the complete public brief and coach card for each scene.
- `index.json` and `catalog.md` are derived outputs generated by the loader's `build-index` command after content validation; they are not hand-authored curriculum sources.

Coverage is selected Go backend, trading-system and EVM/Web3 engineering. It does not imply coverage of every Web3 occupation, chain or product.

## Self-contained teaching cards

Each scene includes explicit fictional context and architecture assumptions, candidate and interviewer tasks, stages, bilingual recognition terms, reusable English expression seeds, registered concepts and source locators. The `coach_only` object supplies one English anchor, responsive depth-tagged follow-ups, an original concise English reference answer, role-specific criteria, acceptable alternatives, misconceptions, a fictional candidate and a changed-condition transfer drill.

The installed runtime does **not** need the original `web3-go/` directory or source lookup to conduct a session. Source paths document provenance, not runtime imports. The cards do not reproduce full source answers, and the English model is a conditional teaching explanation rather than an invented employment story.

Public context and assumptions describe the exercise's architecture, available capabilities, business facts and success contract. They are not a recovery implementation to memorize. Terms and expression seeds support language preparation without substituting a complete technical solution.

## Public preview and coach material

Use the existing loader relative to the installed skill directory. A normal `show` returns the learner-safe brief. Load the same scene with explicit `--coach` only for coaching; do not expose the anchor bank, model answer, rubric, complications or transfer drill in the preview. This is **pedagogical separation**, not a security boundary: someone inspecting a local JSON file can read its coach material.

Before starting, agree the role, English level, technical depth, duration, pressure and architecture assumptions, then wait for readiness. English proficiency, engineering depth and interview pressure are independent settings. Pressure is fair probing, not abuse or permission to change facts.

- **5–10 minutes:** one anchor and at most one useful responsive follow-up.
- **20 minutes:** one anchor and two or three responsive follow-ups.
- **40 minutes:** deeper failure analysis and, if useful, one transfer question.

Ask one speakable question per turn. Follow-up triggers depend on the actual answer; the list is not a compulsory interrogation sequence. Every scene has a foundation follow-up and foundation rubric opportunity. Criteria above the selected depth are not implied requirements, and an unelicited concept is `not_observed` rather than failure.

For candidate practice, the coach interviews the learner. For interviewer practice, the coach plays the clearly fictional incomplete candidate and reveals details in response to the learner's questions. Assess the learner's questions, diagnosis and evaluation reasoning—not the coach candidate's answer. Do not count coach repairs or repeated model answers as independent learner evidence.

## Evidence and assistance

Report language and technical findings separately. Technical observations use `met`, `partial`, `unmet` or `not_observed`, with actual learner evidence or an explicitly labeled evidence summary. Record language support independently from technical support; a wording hint that supplies a missing engineering idea is also technical assistance. A directed missing-issue probe may be technical `probe`; a neutral request to justify the learner's existing claim need not be.

Archive only sanitized learner-visible facts and observed evidence under the existing contract. Do not archive coach-only answers or source dumps as learner work. Contextual observations remain qualified by role, technical depth, scene assumptions and assistance. They do not create a mastery score, hiring recommendation, professional certification or pronunciation assessment from text.

## Architecture boundaries matter

The scenes intentionally do not share one universal architecture:

- Custodial spot scenes separate matching decisions, account entitlements and read projections. A local account transaction does not silently span another service.
- Wallet-controlled AMM, RFQ and signed-intent scenes do not import exchange-style internal reservations as wallet locks.
- The broadcast sample uses ordinary-EOA Ethereum L1 withdrawal semantics and explicitly trusted finalized evidence. It does not prescribe a universal confirmation count.
- The Go ownership scene discusses a durable result-commit boundary. The replay scene explicitly switches to a pure command-state-machine recovery architecture; their recovery authorities must not be silently mixed.
- Margin examples are simplified teaching products. The margin-isolation scene specifies `equity <= maintenance`; the liquidation scene specifies strict `equity < maintenance`. These are declared scene rules, not competing claims about a universal trigger.
- RFQ uses strict `block.timestamp < deadline`; the signed-intent scene uses inclusive `block.timestamp <= deadline`. Preserve each stated contract when probing equality.
- The AMM arithmetic example uses no-fee real-number reasoning, not deployed integer-contract results. Fees, decimals, rounding and supported token behavior need explicit rules in a real implementation.

A changed assumption is a transfer exercise, not permission to retroactively judge the learner under a different contract.

## Provenance, privacy and limits

The source mappings refer to user-provided local Markdown material under `web3-go/`, with actual chapter headings or locators recorded in each scene's `sources`. These are plain relative provenance paths, not promises that original material is distributed in an installed package. Source material remains unchanged by this curriculum work.

All organizations, roles, configurations and scenarios are fictional or explicitly simulated. Teaching amounts are not live prices. Never request private keys, seed phrases, production credentials, undisclosed customer data or confidential employer documents. Distinguish a hypothetical explanation from a learner's real experience.

Content/schema validation, distribution checks and source-locator checks establish defined structural properties only. They do not establish a comprehensive technical audit, a security audit, production readiness, benchmark performance, exercised concurrency or crash guarantees, or real learner validation. Valid alternatives remain acceptable when they preserve the declared guarantees or explicitly change and justify the architecture. Any production use requires independent engineering, product, security and operational review.
